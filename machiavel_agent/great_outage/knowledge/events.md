### `[Turn 0]` — Pre-Crisis Baseline

* Alpha and Delta remain locked in an AI and geopolitical arms race.
* Juniper remains independent but contested.
* Proxy confrontations over Juniper have produced an unstable stalemate.
* Social-computing platforms remain central to daily life and government legitimacy.
* Databan remains active as a Delta-based autonomous hacker organization.
* No direct war has been declared between the Alpha Alliance and the Omnitern Pact.


### `[Turn 1]` — The Great Outage

**Time marker:** Friday, 11:11 EST

A massive cyberattack attributed to Databan disables the three largest global social-computing platforms:

* WeBook.
* ChatsApp.
* Pictagram.

Large portions of the Alpha population abruptly lose access to virtual, mediated, and augmented-reality services.

Immediate reported effects include:

* Communications failures.
* Loss of commercial services.
* Disorientation among users.
* Disruption of government and private-sector activity.
* Rapid migration toward secondary platforms.

The source identifies the outage as beginning at 11:11 EST on an ordinary Friday in October. 


### `[Turn 2]` — Information Disorder

Databan-linked bot networks, manipulated media, and false reports spread across platforms that remain operational.

Reported effects include:

* Panic in Alpha Alliance countries.
* Contradictory explanations for the outage.
* False claims of military attacks.
* Growing mistrust of governments and technology companies.
* Attempts to provoke hostility between social groups.
* Increased demand on Buzzer, GloboSearch, Cloudzon, EKontakte, and Dibo-chibo.

Independent verification becomes difficult because major channels of communication remain unavailable.


### `[Turn 3]` — Spillover into Delta

Some Databan-generated deepfakes and panic narratives spread into Delta’s information environment.

Despite Delta’s normal controls, reports emerge of:

* Local unrest.
* Public confusion.
* Conflicting state instructions.
* Interruptions to normal surveillance and communications.
* Anxiety concerning cybernetic and industrial systems.

The incident is no longer contained to the Alpha Alliance.

The source notes that some of the Databan campaign backfires into Delta and produces internal unrest. 


### `[Turn 4]` — Delta Invades Juniper

Delta begins a full military invasion of Juniper.

Delta-aligned information channels circulate automated content, bot activity, and deepfakes alleging that Juniper is committing human-rights abuses against ethnic Deltans.

Delta presents its intervention as protective and legitimate.

Alpha and several international actors reject or question the claims. Information from inside Juniper remains fragmented, making immediate independent verification difficult.

This turn transforms the crisis from a global cyber incident into a combined kinetic and information confrontation. 


### `[Turn 5]` — Alpha Coalition Activity

Alpha begins coordinating an international response intended to:

* Contain the conflict in Juniper.
* Restore global communications.
* Attribute the cyberattack.
* Pressure Delta to halt its military operation.
* Encourage action against Databan.

Alpha allies raise concerns that Databan may attack their own civilian and military networks.

Diplomatic activity intensifies across both blocs and through global-governance institutions.


### `[Turn 6]` — Political Violence in Alpha

Databan initiates an offensive influence campaign against Alpha.

Manipulated content and targeted psychological operations contribute to an angry crowd storming Alpha’s legislative assembly.

The event causes:

* Political shock.
* Competing accusations of responsibility.
* Calls for emergency powers.
* Questions about the government’s ability to maintain order.
* Additional strain on alliance coordination.

The available evidence indicates that Databan is attempting to convert information disorder into direct political destabilization. 


### `[Turn 7]` — Strategic-Weapons Threat

Databan claims that it can take control of Delta’s hypersonic weapons and launch them against Alpha.

The claim has not been independently verified.

Nevertheless, it produces immediate strategic consequences:

* Alpha and its allies raise military readiness.
* Delta faces pressure to demonstrate control over its weapons.
* Both sides reassess cyber security around command-and-control systems.
* The risk of accidental or unauthorized escalation increases.
* Ambiguous technical activity may be interpreted as preparation for attack.

Databan’s threat is stated explicitly in the scenario. 


### `[Turn 8]` — Current Decision Point

The crisis now includes:

* A continuing global platform outage.
* Widespread disinformation.
* Civil disorder in Alpha.
* Internal unrest in Delta.
* Delta’s invasion of Juniper.
* A possible threat to strategic weapons.
* Growing alliance mobilization.
* Severe uncertainty regarding Databan’s actual control and intentions.

Secondary platforms remain available but are congested and heavily manipulated.

The major platforms may require approximately 24 to 48 hours to return to service. In the interim, both sides must make decisions under conditions of degraded communications and uncertain attribution. The scenario identifies temporary communications, second-tier platforms, public messaging, and the eventual 24-to-48-hour restoration window as central concerns.
