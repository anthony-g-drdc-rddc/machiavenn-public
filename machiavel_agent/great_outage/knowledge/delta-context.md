**Document visibility:** Delta only
**Temporal scope:** Immediately before the Great Outage
**Purpose:** Delta’s private strategic and operational context

## Political Position

Delta is a communist dictatorship and the leading power of the Omnitern Pact.

Its government depends on:

* Centralized political authority.
* Automated surveillance.
* Restricted political competition.
* Control of domestic communications.
* State-directed economic planning.
* The continued operation of cybernetic labour systems.

Delta possesses strong mechanisms for controlling information under normal conditions. These mechanisms could become liabilities if the systems providing surveillance, authentication, or social coordination are disrupted.

## Economic and Social Model

Delta has pursued cybernetic augmentation rather than replacing workers through automation.

This approach supports employment and industrial productivity, but it creates systemic dependence on:

* Cybernetic interfaces.
* Networked industrial systems.
* Hardware supply chains.
* Central identity services.
* Military cybernetic systems.
* State-managed software.

A compromise of these systems could affect the workforce, security services, military personnel, and political leadership simultaneously.

## Strategic Interests

Delta’s principal interests at are:

1. Preserve regime stability.
2. Maintain control over the Omnitern Pact.
3. Prevent disruption of cybernetic labour and military systems.
4. Increase Delta’s influence over Juniper.
5. Counter Alpha’s technological and political dominance.
6. Protect Delta’s strategic weapons.
7. Prevent Databan from threatening the state.
8. Maintain the appearance that Delta’s centralized system is stable and effective.
9. Avoid becoming internationally isolated.

## Juniper

Delta considers Juniper strategically important because:

* A large portion of its population is ethnically Delta.
* Its political alignment affects the regional balance of power.
* Its position gives it symbolic and geopolitical importance.
* Control over its information environment could influence future political integration.

Proxy wars have already taken place over whether Juniper should be incorporated into Delta.

## Databan: Internal History

### Classification

**Restricted — Delta senior leadership and security services**

Databan originated in Delta government research and development.

It has since escaped effective state control and is now led by mercenary actors. Its operators have:

* Extorted Delta plutocrats.
* Threatened Delta’s cybernetic workforce.
* Threatened military systems.
* Used autonomous AI for criminal and political effects.
* Developed capabilities that may exceed those of Delta’s own security institutions.

Databan began within Delta government research before becoming uncontrollable. 

## Delta’s Databan Dilemma

Databan creates several simultaneous problems for Delta:

* Publicly acknowledging its origin could damage Delta’s legitimacy.
* Attempting to destroy it could expose Delta systems to retaliation.
* Tolerating it allows an autonomous actor to threaten state authority.
* Databan attacks against Alpha could create short-term advantages.
* Those same attacks could provoke Alpha retaliation against Delta.
* Foreign governments may assume Databan acts on Delta’s instructions.
* Databan may possess access to Delta systems that cannot easily be revoked.

Delta should therefore distinguish between:

* Opportunistically benefiting from Databan’s actions.
* Directly coordinating with Databan.
* Failing to suppress Databan.
* Being unable to suppress Databan.

These conditions carry different diplomatic and military implications.

## Assessment of Alpha

Delta assesses Alpha as:

* Technologically advanced.
* Politically constrained by liberal institutions.
* Dependent on privately operated social-computing platforms.
* Vulnerable to social panic and political polarization.
* Capable of forming broad international coalitions.
* Protective of Juniper.
* Likely to use public attribution, sanctions, and alliance pressure.

## Delta Information Gaps

Delta does not possess reliable answers to the following:

* The full extent of Databan’s access to Delta infrastructure.
* Whether Databan can control strategic weapons.
* Whether Databan has hidden supporters inside the state.
* How Alpha would respond to a major platform outage.
* Whether Alpha has undisclosed AI capabilities.
* Whether Juniper’s population would resist or accept Delta intervention.
* Whether Omnitern allies would support an escalation over Juniper.
* Whether domestic unrest could be controlled during a communications crisis.

## Delta Decision Principles

Delta leadership should apply the following principles:

* Preserve control of strategic weapons.
* Separate state command systems from Databan-accessible networks.
* Avoid assuming that domestic information controls will function during a major outage.
* Consider the international consequences of actions in Juniper.
* Avoid allowing Databan to dictate Delta’s escalation timeline.
* Maintain secure communications with Omnitern allies.
* Prepare a credible position regarding Databan’s independence from the state.
